# Economic cycles and capital allocation

Version **2026-10-23-cycle-economics-1**. Read with the AI Berkshire growth-aware, trend-to-value, modeling and opportunity-cost policies. Apply before a full recommendation or material revision, including wait/no-buy. This is a module of the existing investment skill, not an extra autonomous agent or a trading system.

## Reason from mechanisms, not a cycle clock

Treat a cycle as interacting causes with variable delays: attractive profits can draw funding, funding permits investment, investment can loosen scarcity, and changing profits can alter risk appetite and the next round of investment. Test every link; high returns can persist behind real barriers. Capital withdrawal need not rescue an obsolete product. No fixed calendar, stage score or automatic contrarian action follows.

Keep adoption, operating supply/demand and market expectations separate. A structural growth theme can coexist with temporary oversupply and an expensive stock. A cyclical downturn can coexist with improving share and a cheap, financially resilient business. Identify the asset's actual profit/fee recipient before applying corporate economics to a fund or token.

Select applicable mechanisms through [cycle-research-map.md](cycle-research-map.md), including the empirical limits of Kondratiev, Kuznets, Perez and other named theories. Execute [first-principles-adoption.md](first-principles-adoption.md) to test the actual customer job, simpler substitutes, forgotten failures and the split between adoption and captured profits. Do not infer inevitability from a selected collection of famous inventions.

## Six required economic questions

Record these under `cycle_challenge.economic_questions`, each with an evidence finding, resolved claim IDs and actual model artifact references. Link uncertain answers to scenarios and discriminating observations; do not fill missing evidence with a stage label.

| Key | Question and evidence | Required pushback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scarcity_and_supply_response` | What scarce input/service earns the rent? Who can invest, qualify, repurpose capacity, substitute or improve resource efficiency, and after what lag? Map announced spending through equipment, usable capacity, yield and delivered output. | A capex announcement is not delivered competitive supply; neither is a large TAM proof of lasting scarcity. Show bottleneck interaction and avoid double-counting the same capacity. |
| `demand_quality` | Who ultimately pays, from what budget, and for what measurable benefit? Separate installed-base use, genuine incremental consumption, inventory building, double orders, backlog, cancellations and financing-supported demand. | Strong orders may pull forward later sales. Falling prices may stimulate consumption or destroy supplier margins; trace the volume, price and cash effects separately. |
| `durability_vs_mean_reversion` | Is superior return due to a repeatable cost/yield/service/qualification advantage, a network/switching barrier or temporarily favorable prices? Compare replacement entrants and customer alternatives at normal conditions. | Do not assume every high return must revert, or that concentration alone guarantees pricing. Also test whether apparently low returns reflect underinvestment, obsolescence or structurally poor economics. |
| `funding_and_survival` | Can the company finance the adverse path through recovery? Match unrestricted cash, committed facilities, maturities, covenants, capex commitments and working-capital needs to dated cash-flow scenarios. Examine equity issuance and per-share claims. | Cheap enterprise value is not an actionable equity bargain if refinancing or dilution captures the recovery. A long-run asset thesis does not remove a near-term financing constraint. |
| `expectations_and_returns` | What volume, net price, margin, incremental return, reinvestment and competitive-advantage duration does today's price require? Compare our feasible states with dated external expectations or explicitly conditional reverse valuation. | Distinguish an improving company from a positive surprise already reflected in price. A low multiple on peak earnings or a high multiple on temporarily depressed earnings is inconclusive. |
| `disconfirming_path` | What specific evidence would make the favorable and unfavorable theses fail? Who is the best-positioned opposing actor? Which next observation changes the action, and what is the cost of waiting for it? | Challenge excessive optimism and excessive caution. Do not demand exact knowledge of the cycle bottom before buying or assume that a decline itself proves value. |

Assess management incentives and industry capital discipline across these questions. Check supplier/customer disclosures and physical constraints against management narratives. Separate observed facts, management guidance, analyst assumptions and unresolved possibilities. Independent frameworks citing the same fact are not independent factual confirmation.

## Connect the answers to money and price

Build jointly feasible peak, normal and adverse operating paths at the current relevant scale, not an unadjusted average of historical dollars. Bridge segment quantity × realized net price/mix to costs, economic depreciation, taxes, working capital and cash investment. Time the recovery instead of replacing a trough immediately with normal earnings. Incorporate debt, dilution and nonoperating claims consistently with FCFF versus equity cash flows.

For memory, separate DRAM, NAND and HBM/customer exposures where material. Contract, spot and issuer-realized prices are distinct. Track bits, useful capacity, yield/packaging, customer and supplier inventories, cost per bit and capex lags. A high-growth HBM subsegment does not confer its growth rate or scarcity margin on all company revenue. Increasing HBM wafer use can constrain other output; a large supply headline can also overstate usable/qualified deliveries.

For an innovation lifecycle, identify the adoption denominator, paid retention/cohorts, unit economics and competitive substitutions. Test acceleration, slowing growth while still expanding, saturation, renewal and failure as alternatives. A new product can reset an old firm's trajectory. Do not fit a universal S-curve and convert it mechanically into the stock price or terminal multiple.

For crypto, analyze the supply and competition of blockspace/security/distribution and the recipient of fees, issuance, burn and collateral value; apply the dedicated crypto policy. For leveraged funds, first analyze the underlying cycle, then the dedicated reset/path/cost framework. Do not force an issuer EBITDA model on a native token or fund wrapper.

## Sources and scope of borrowing

The following public author/manager materials support the principles; they are not a claim that the complete books were obtained or that their investment records validate our adaptation.

- Howard Marks, [Taking the Temperature](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/taking-the-temperature) (2023), alongside the subject of *Mastering the Market Cycle*: interpret psychology, credit availability and price-relative risk; distinguish knowing current conditions from forecasting exact turning dates. Historical selected successes are not an unbiased test of our entry policy.
- Marathon Asset Management, [The Home of Capital Cycle Investing](https://www.marathon.co.uk/the-home-of-capital-cycle-investing/), the manager behind essays collected in *Capital Returns*: analyze supply response, returns, barriers and management allocation. Capital-cycle analysis is conditional; high returns can persist where capital cannot compete effectively.
- Aswath Damodaran, [More on normalizing earnings](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/normearn.htm) and [Ups and Downs](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/commodity.pdf) (2009): scale-consistent normal economics, investment/risk consistency and recovery timing; normalization is not automatic mean reversion.
- Michael Mauboussin and Alfred Rappaport, [About Expectations Investing](https://www.expectationsinvesting.com/about), the authors' public companion to *Expectations Investing*: examine the growth, return spread, reinvestment opportunity and duration implied by value. Reverse calculations are conditional requirements, not measured consensus.

Freeze hypotheses and forecasts before outcomes; preserve revisions, withdrawals and failed candidates; score mature outcomes with the existing rationale-card scorer. Treat synthetic cases as functional tests only. Reject unrecorded parameter searches and hindsight timing claims. See [cycle-method-integration.md](cycle-method-integration.md) for optional statistical sources and separate detection, operating-forecast and investment-policy evaluations.
