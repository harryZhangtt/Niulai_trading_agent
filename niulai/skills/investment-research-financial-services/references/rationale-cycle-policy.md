3. Build the card and the cycle challenge below from the existing evidence/model. Reuse existing work. Use unknown/mixed when identification is weak; lack of data is not evidence of no change. Quantities in the visual must bind by JSON pointer to actual hashed model outputs.

5. Have the existing independent reviewer challenge the cycle/valuation thesis and inspect source links, model numbers, graph and exact report. Supply `visual_inspection: {method: rendered_html|rendered_markdown|native_preview, finding: ...}` only after actual viewing. 6. Run `rationale_guard.py validate --record RECORD.json`. Finalization and full completion call the same validator. Persist the lock, card, diagram, review and canonical report with actual receipts/readbacks, preferably as members of the existing research archive. Failed validation returns rejection, not a completed recommendation. A diagram or reviewer string is not proof of economic truth or a platform-wide unbypassable stop hook.

## Required visual argument

Use eight connected nodes: what the business/asset does; moat and fragility; revenue-to-owner-value capture; the revenue-and-profit beat test; market expectations versus our view; lifecycle/cycle position; catalyst timing and magnitude; action, entry and downside. For crypto/funds adapt operating revenue to the relevant fee/underlying/payoff economics and label that adaptation. Do not invent a company inside a token or fund wrapper.

Test a double beat instead of presupposing one: define the period, revenue measure and profit/margin/owner-cash measure, identify dated expectations or clearly labeled reverse-implied conditions, and show whether each is supported, mixed or not established. The visual can conclude that a fine business is fully priced, the expected catalyst is already discounted, or a defensible wait/no-buy is best. A positive-alpha quota is forbidden.

Bind entry bounds and valuation horizon to model outputs; bind catalyst timing/magnitude where identifiable. Each numeric binding supplies `artifact_id`, `pointer`, `units`, `period`, `units_pointer` and `period_pointer`; the latter pointers must resolve to matching metadata in the actual output. Endpoint definitions must agree; timing units are months. Use `{{metric_key}}` placeholders for quantitative estimates in visual prose; the renderer inserts verified values/units. Where no entry is feasible, use `entry_feasibility: none`, a model-bound tested domain and reason rather than inventing a tier. Where a magnitude or timing cannot be identified, record why, the observable trigger and sensitivity/switching conditions. Do not hide that uncertainty with a precise date or forced probability. State invalidation and the evidence that would change the action.

The `beat_test` states period, revenue metric, profit metric and `basis: dated_consensus|price_implied|unavailable`. Known bases require model-bound revenue/profit forecasts and corresponding expectations with matched metric definitions. With an unavailable basis the visual's double-beat status must be not-established. Reverse-implied expectations are not externally observed consensus.

## Three separate clocks

Execute [cycle-capital-allocation.md](cycle-capital-allocation.md) for the six evidence-linked economic questions and applicable deterministic calculation packet. Read [cycle-method-integration.md](cycle-method-integration.md) before choosing numerical cycle/trend methods. Its source registry distinguishes adopted reviewer/data contracts from candidate, uninstalled statistical adapters; it records availability, leakage, benchmark and promotion tests. No optional package becomes a hidden blocker or an executed method by being named here.

- **Adoption/business lifecycle:** inception, accelerating adoption, decelerating growth, maturity, decline, mixed or unknown. Identify the unit, penetration denominator, cohort retention, budgets and unit economics. A fitted S-curve is not proof that a technology must follow it; segment/product lifecycles can differ, restart or fail.
- **Industry operating cycle:** inventory, utilization/yield, delivered capacity, customer demand, realized prices, costs, capital additions and lead times. Separate a structural demand shift from stockbuilding, pull-forward and delayed supply response. Use levels and rates of change with compatible units; accelerating growth and growing but decelerating demand are different states.
- **Market/expectations cycle:** dated operating revisions, reverse-implied growth/margins/duration, discount rates, valuation and relative price behavior. Prices may turn before reported results. A strong trend is not proof of undervaluation; a falling price is not proof of a bargain.

For cyclical/capital-intensive businesses compare peak, normalized and adverse cash-flow states, including consistent capex, working capital, taxes, debt and risk assumptions. Low peak-cycle P/E is not automatically cheap. Where earnings are negative/near-zero use an economically meaningful cash/asset framework rather than a meaningless multiple. Model stock returns from both per-share fundamentals and repricing, including financing/distributions; do not extrapolate an operating S-curve directly into a stock-price path.

## Memory implementation

Disaggregate DRAM, NAND and HBM/product/customer exposures when material. Track bits/units, realized price and mix separately, cost per bit, yields/utilization, supplier and customer inventories, contract versus spot coverage, actual wafer/packaging capacity, qualified supply and lagged capex. An HBM allocation can constrain other products; do not count the same wafers twice. Contract prices, spot quotes, management forecasts and actual shipments are different observations. Date and source each; undisclosed observations remain explicit gaps.

Test at least the economic alternatives supported by evidence: persistent tightness from real demand, temporary shortage/pull-forward, and a supply/efficiency/substitution response. The card requires competing hypotheses and discriminating next observations, not an unsupported exact cycle peak date. Model end-demand/bit growth together with net supply, realized ASP/mix, cost learning, capex and cash conversion. Reconcile management incentives and contrary supplier/customer evidence. Use joint feasible states, not independent bullish uplifts.

## Adversarial review and entry

Ask: What has the price already capitalized? Does the apparent margin advantage survive normal supply? Which customer can insource/substitute or negotiate? Does efficiency reduce scarce units per workload? Would an earnings beat still disappoint elevated expectations? What loss/dilution can occur before the thesis matures? The reviewer can reject both a bullish story and an excessively conservative wait. Reuse the equal-budget now/staged/wait comparison and its nonfill/invalidation/cash rules; show action-flip thresholds.

Do not install a second trading executor to obtain a dissenting opinion. Adapt the bounded bull/bear/risk challenge pattern from TradingAgents within the current native reviewer; use FinRobot as a modeling/reference comparison, not evidence that those packages are installed or have proven alpha. Price momentum is a separate testable input, not a replacement for valuation or a universal timing veto.

## Frozen records and tests

Each card freezes its vintage/cohort, thesis family, data cutoff, method/source/model hashes, hypotheses, cycle indicators, action and at least one material resolvable forecast. Specify units, target period, mean/median/binary/interval functional, resolution date/source/rule and origin (prospective versus reconstructed). Source values from actual model bindings. Never relabel scenario endpoints as calibrated quantiles. Append revisions and outcomes through existing revisioned evidence/assessment storage, preserving the original card hash and entry policies. No new scheduler is required.

Use `rationale_guard.py score --card CARD.json --outcomes OBSERVATIONS.json --output SCORES.json` for deterministic scoped forecast scores. Outcomes must identify the exact forecast and card hash, actual source and availability date. Missing/unmatured outcomes remain pending and are counted. Report signed mean errors/mean squared error for mean forecasts, absolute error for medians, Brier loss for binary events, and coverage/width/interval score for true intervals. Do not pool unscaled dollars with percentages or treat correlated updates/tickers as independent. A per-card score is not a statistically significant calibration estimate or causal attribution.

Keep published withdrawals in the evaluation cohort whenever resolvable. Track rejected/deferred names and nonfills, not just winners. Compare methodology changes on frozen functional fixtures first, then untouched time-forward cohorts including adverse cycles. Record every tested variant, eligibility, missing outcomes, token/runtime cost, material errors and benchmark-relative policy outcomes. Do not tune a cycle stage, hurdle or probability to fit the user's preferred price. Sparse data permits descriptive errors and explicit limits, not an optimized general rule.
