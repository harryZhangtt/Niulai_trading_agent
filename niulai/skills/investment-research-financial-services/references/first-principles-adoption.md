# First-principles demand and the missing failures

Policy **2026-10-23-first-principles-1**. Apply to new full recommendations and material revisions alongside the asset-specific workflow. This is a critic and economics extension, not a thesis that every innovation fails or that successful adoption must produce a winning security.

## Start with the job, not the technology

Identify the end customer's problem, payer and budget; define the required accuracy, latency, reliability, throughput, legal acceptability and service boundary. Compare the status quo, the simplest sufficient alternative, and the proposed technology on that same job. An alternative can be manual work, ordinary software, workflow redesign, another protocol, a customer-built system or doing nothing. If two alternatives are genuinely identical, explain why instead of inventing a competitor. Describe which customer cohorts benefit and which do not.

Measure monetizable customer benefit after recurring fees, integration, supervision, error losses and switching costs on a consistent annual basis. Time saved is not necessarily a cash saving: require evidence of avoided hiring, redeployed productive capacity, increased realized revenue, lower losses or willingness to pay. Define overlapping cost categories to avoid charging the same labor twice. Annualization is a diagnostic; financing, timing and discounting belong in the full model. Unavailable inputs remain unavailable or explicitly assumed, with sensitivities.

Separate trials, subsidized use, paid conversion, renewal and profitable repeat use. Count the eligible population and actual retained cohorts, not downloads or a conveniently expanding TAM. User surplus, customer spending, supplier revenue, owner cash and security-holder returns are distinct quantities. Trace bargaining power, complementary assets, competition, distribution fees, reinvestment, dilution and timing through the existing model. Never add the same technology benefit to both volume and margins without reconciliation.

## Challenge the success story and the skeptical story

Use four alternative outcome families: abandonment; durable niche; broad adoption with weak supplier capture; broad adoption with strong supplier capture. Define their subject, horizon and boundaries so the proposed partition is mutually exclusive. A mature business can have a small abandonment weight, but zero requires a reason. Do not assign equal weights simply to fill a table. Unestimated probabilities remain null; judgment weights are assumptions, not measured frequencies. Do not multiply marginal probabilities for adoption, execution and capture as though their dependencies vanished. Existing trend-to-value and valuation models handle joint cash-flow scenarios.

Before examining winners, freeze the reference-class inclusion rule, cohort start, outcome horizon and outcome definition. Search for abandoned, acquired, failed, forgotten, niche and unresolved candidates. An acquisition is not automatically a failure or investor success. Avoid conditioning the denominator on survival, listing, press visibility or eventual scale. Record missing names and missing outcomes; a collection of famous anecdotes is not a measurable base rate. Preserve failed forecasts, withdrawn theses and changed cohort definitions rather than overwriting them.

If a population denominator is unknown, the script must return not estimable. If the denominator is known but outcomes are unresolved, show identification bounds and unresolved counts; these are not confidence intervals or calibrated investment probabilities. Even a complete cohort needs a human comparability review. Do not force an invented quota of failures, and do not use an old negative study as timeless proof against an improving technology.

For each material thesis precommit a dated observation, measurement source, favorable threshold, unfavorable threshold and resulting model/action reconsideration. Explain which observation would change the critic's own mind. Update both sides symmetrically. Failure to see news is not confirmation; a test that cannot discriminate between competing explanations adds little.

## Cycle philosophy is a hypothesis generator

Read [Cycle research map](cycle-research-map.md) for business, inventory, investment, financial, demographic/infrastructure, technology and market-expectations mechanisms. Kondratiev and Perez can suggest questions about infrastructure, institutions, finance and complementary applications. They cannot establish that today's technology belongs among the eventual transformative winners, a universal phase duration or an investable turning date. Separate secular change, seasonal variation and endogenous feedback. Test the actual customer and capital mechanism before naming the stage.

## Professional foundations and scope

- Denrell (2003), [Vicarious Learning, Undersampling of Failure, and the Myths of Management](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/vicarious-learning-undersampling-failure-myths-management): observed survivors can create misleading associations. This motivates cohort discipline, not an automatic discount for every growth company.
- Hall and Khan (2003), [Adoption of New Technology](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9730): adoption involves heterogeneous uncertain benefits and adoption costs; a discovery alone does not establish diffusion.
- Teece (1986), [Profiting from technological innovation](https://doi.org/10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2): appropriability and complementary assets affect who captures innovation profits. It is not a numerical probability estimator.
- Camuffo and colleagues (2024), [A scientific approach to entrepreneurial decision-making: Large-scale replication and extension](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.3580): test explicit hypotheses and revise or terminate projects on evidence. Entrepreneurial experiments do not establish an investment alpha claim for this implementation.
- Perez (2002), [Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital, introduction](https://carlotaperez.org/wp-content/downloads/books/btr-en/PEREZ_TRFC_Intro.pdf): a historical interpretive framework for major technological revolutions. Its selected revolutions do not supply the success base rate for all inventions. Only the accessible introductory material is claimed reviewed here.
