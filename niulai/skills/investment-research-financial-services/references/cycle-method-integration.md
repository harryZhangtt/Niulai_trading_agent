## Data before a cycle label

Keep three distinct clocks: adoption/penetration, the industry's operating cycle, and market expectations/valuation. An asset can have accelerating adoption, rising industry inventory and an expensive stock simultaneously. No single universal S-curve or cycle duration applies to all three.

An operating-series observation records security/segment, metric definition, units, measurement period, publication/availability timestamp, source identity/version and revision vintage. At cutoff t use only observations and revisions actually available by t. A quarter-end date is not the filing date. Missing history is a stated limit; never fabricate older vintages from today's revised history.

For memory keep DRAM/NAND/HBM separate when material; distinguish spot/contract/realized ASP and product mix, bits versus dollars, qualified versus delivered capacity, and supplier versus customer inventory. Reconcile capex/yield/packaging lags and wafer allocation. A spot-price chart alone cannot establish a company's ASP or owner-profit forecast.

## Integration path

The lightweight first-principles extension is now local: `adoption_checks.py` computes declared customer alternatives and denominator bounds; `cycle_vintage_checks.py` selects a declared point-in-time data scope. Their interfaces/examples live in the adjacent test files. Neither estimates a cycle or installs the candidate packages above. Use [cycle-research-map.md](cycle-research-map.md) for the professional literature and method disagreements and [first-principles-adoption.md](first-principles-adoption.md) for the enforced critic evidence. The vintage helper's selected-view hash excludes later information; preserve the full input packet hash separately for provenance.

1. **Existing native research, now mandatory:** freeze evidence and competing cycle hypotheses, normalize peak/normal/adverse economics, reverse the price into its required growth/margins, and compare buy-now/stage/wait under the existing equal-budget rules. The rationale guard already checks the linked artifacts and reviewer coverage.
2. **Numerical shadow adapter:** the small frozen-origin evaluator described below is now implemented. A structural/regime estimator is still optional and not installed by this change. Begin with one only if data support it; use change-point diagnostics only for an identified instability question. An agent gathers and interprets primary evidence; an explicit Python calculation runs the chosen estimator. A result stores method/version, full input hash, data/fit cutoff, available-at maximum, train window, transformations, parameters/seed, convergence status, state/forecast output and limitations. Do not invent an agent inside a script.
3. **Bounded challenge:** a separate reviewer tests durable pricing power versus temporary scarcity, supply/efficiency response, peak-earnings cheapness, priced-in optimism and the cost of waiting. It may uphold buy, stage, wait or no-buy. Reuse the existing model and source archive; challenge the assumptions that can change action.
4. **Promotion only after evidence:** adopt a numerical output in valuation weights or entry policy only after time-ordered evaluation improves a predefined decision-relevant metric against simple baselines without an unacceptable increase in loss, cost or fragility. Until then show shadow results separately; do not change alert levels on that basis.

## Required tests for any later numerical adapter

- **Future-tail test:** appending later data cannot alter a frozen historical decision, fitted parameters or original artifact hash. Retrospective revised estimates are stored under new identities and labeled as such.
- **Vintage and availability test:** late filings, revised macro series, delisted names and missing outcomes remain correctly handled. No survivorship-only universe or retrospective consensus substitution.
- **Decision/execution time test:** a decision using after-close disclosures cannot transact at that same session's close. Use an explicit next-tradable observation and execution convention; preserve the old scoring version rather than resume a mixed-version backtest. Historical LLM weights may also encode later outcomes: retrospective agent debate is an error review, not an unbiased predictive test.
- **Window/transform test:** imputation, scaling, outlier limits, change-point penalties, lag selection and model choice are fitted or chosen only within each training fold. Use time-ordered evaluation; account for overlapping horizons. No random shuffled train/test split for these dependent observations.
- **Nonconvergence and missing-dependency test:** record the exact failure. A numerical diagnostic may be unavailable without blocking a separately supported fundamental conclusion; it may not be replaced silently or counted as executed. If the recommendation materially depends on that diagnostic, leave that recommendation incomplete.
- **Cycle stress:** structural growth plus inventory overshoot; prices still rising but growth decelerating; capex/supply arrives late; peak earnings mask normalized expense; new technology resets the product mix; secular decline; contradictory indicators. Sparse data must allow unknown rather than force a stage.
- **Fair comparison:** freeze cohort, targets, horizons, losses, transforms and a small candidate family before inspecting outcomes. Record every tried variant. Compare operating forecasts with naive/seasonal-naive baselines and entry policies on equal capital, cash yield, distributions, costs, nonfills and invalidation rules. A detection score is not an equity-return score.
- **Score and review:** preserve the original rationale-card hash and forecast functional; use the existing scoped scorer for mature observable outcomes. Keep pending/missing/withdrawn counts. Use grouped/time-blocked uncertainty if enough independent history exists. Do not claim calibrated probabilities or superior alpha from synthetic fixtures or a handful of correlated semiconductor names.

## Lightweight frozen-origin comparison

Run `references/niulai-workflow/scripts/cycle_eval_checks.py --input PACKET.json --output RESULT.json` before claiming a numerical cycle forecast improves a simple baseline. The adjacent test's `example_packet()` is the versioned interface and a clearly labeled synthetic example. The helper reuses `cycle_vintage_checks.calculate` and `rationale_guard.score`; it adds no estimator dependency, data service, scheduler or trading engine.

Freeze the expected case/member roster, target periods, one candidate protocol specification, origin/fit/issue cutoffs, forecast functional, cost fields and minimum reporting counts before scoring. For each member/origin, the helper recomputes the available training vintage, verifies its hash and computes a naïve forecast from the last available observation. Supply the frozen candidate forecast or an explained abstention. It rejects omitted roster cases, unregistered or pooled candidate variants, wrong subjects/units, stale training-view hashes, releases unavailable at the stated fit time, premature/future outcomes and overlapping evaluation horizons for one member. Supply genuinely chronological training/decision windows; transformations and estimator code still require independent review because declared timestamps alone cannot prove the model was actually trained that way.

Use one metric/unit/functional per packet. Means use the existing squared-error scorer; medians use absolute error. Compare on the exact same resolved cases and report expected, scored, abstained, pending, missing-outcome and insufficient-training counts. Preserve observed success, niche, failure, other and unknown outcome categories independently of whether a prediction was made. A better score obtained after abstaining on all failed businesses is visible as reduced coverage, not a full-cohort success. Caller-supplied categories and the original cohort denominator still need source/reviewer verification.

Report computation dollars for all cases separately from forecast loss. These are not transaction costs or investment P&L; the existing equal-capital entry-policy machinery remains the place for cash yield, fills, funding, fees and stock returns. Small samples return `insufficient_history`. Sufficient declared case counts return only `descriptive_only`, never statistical significance, calibrated probabilities, independent long-wave counts or automatic promotion. Correlated entities and tried variants require separate judgment; this deliberately small implementation does not estimate dependence-robust confidence intervals.

Future revisions may be kept in the complete source packet, but cannot change a frozen origin's selected-view or decision hash. The complete input hash changes when later data are appended; that is expected provenance, not a rewritten forecast. Store scores and subsequent outcomes as new versioned artifacts under the existing run/forecast ledger. A supplied backdated metadata field is not a trustworthy historic receipt. Use authentic saved forecast/vintage evidence for empirical claims.

The current functional regression suite tests the guard, comparison and scoring mechanics. It does **not** establish that cycle forecasts improve real decisions. Full cohort automation, authentic vintage data sourcing, measured latency/token savings and production promotion remain evidence-dependent work; no historical empirical alpha result is claimed by this addition.
