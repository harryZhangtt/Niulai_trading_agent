# Leveraged-fund research policy

Version: **2026-10-18-leveraged-funds-2**. Scope: leveraged/inverse ETFs and ETNs, leveraged closed-end funds, and other funds with material embedded leverage. Apply alongside the growth-aware policy; use this security-specific branch instead of a corporate DCF for the fund itself. Target accuracy and completeness, with no bullish/bearish direction target.

## Contents

1. Identify the actual payoff and investable comparison
2. Establish the return and cost ledger

## 1. Identify the actual payoff and investable comparison

- Read current issuer materials, prospectus/supplements, index methodology and distributions. Record source publication/retrieval times and market-data cutoff. Verify ticker/share class, jurisdiction, benchmark and its change dates, leverage direction/multiple, reset period, fees/waivers, financing and collateral terms, derivatives/counterparties, early-close or acceleration clauses, and split history. Never assume SOXL is literally three times SOXX or any same-sector ETF; separate benchmark mismatch from leverage effects.
- Classify the structure before applying a formula. A daily leveraged ETF targets reset-period exposure, not a promised long-horizon multiple. Weekly/monthly reset funds require returns over that actual reset period and an intraperiod exposure/cost path where material. An ETN adds issuer credit, indicative-value/market-price and call/acceleration risk. A leveraged CEF usually has asset/NAV leverage, borrowing/preferred funding and a changing premium/discount, not daily constant leverage. Derive its asset-to-equity bridge. Options, buffers, covered calls and volatility/futures products have nonlinear payoff/roll/carry mechanics; obtain holdings/payoff inputs rather than forcing a constant-beta formula. Do not claim that the bundled linear-reset calculator covers these structures.
- Specify the investor horizon, holding/rebalancing rule, cash flows, liquidity and existing correlated exposure. Compare the same initial capital and future contribution schedule with an investable unlevered alternative, cash, and, if relevant, a smaller leveraged allocation plus cash. Distinguish initial-exposure matching from continuously rebalanced risk matching. Include cash yield and trading costs for each actual policy.
- Separate existing lump-sum cash deployed gradually from future paychecks invested as earned. State whether contributions are net of living costs, their duration and whether a quoted saving-to-portfolio ratio uses initial or changing wealth. Do not apply lump-sum-versus-cost-averaging success rates to future income or to a different leveraged asset.
- Treat durable future savings as potential loss capacity and an input to lifetime-wealth utility, not evidence that improves the fund's return distribution or entry signal. Assess income/sector dependence and contribution interruptions jointly with market stress. Distinguish diversified lifecycle leverage from concentrated-sector leverage; neither a high saving rate nor a broad-index study identifies the appropriate sector multiple or allocation.

## 2. Establish the return and cost ledger

- Keep benchmark price return, benchmark total return, ETF adjusted market-price total return and fund NAV total return distinct. Align dates/time zones, splits, dividends, distribution reinvestment and holiday calendars. Use a close-to-close series appropriate to the fund's reset valuation time; report missing/stale prices and benchmark-history coverage. Actual fund NAV returns already contain expenses, financing and realized tracking effects: do not subtract those costs again. Investor trading returns additionally reflect entry/exit spreads, premium/discount, commissions and applicable taxes; avoid inventing personal tax assumptions.
- For a forward synthetic model show a dated ledger: management/operating fee net of valid waivers; financing reference rate plus evidenced spread, net financing notional; collateral or cash income; rebalance/transaction costs; residual tracking. A long 3x approximation may finance about 2x NAV, but this is not universal. Swaps/futures, inverse exposure, collateral, short financing and margin can change the ledger. Do not automatically charge `(L-1)*rate` for inverse funds or charge explicit borrowing on top of an all-in swap return.
- Derive gross swap charges and collateral income separately: a schematic swap funding debit per NAV is `swap_notional/NAV * (reference_rate + spread) - collateral_cash/NAV * collateral_yield`, plus other actual financing and expenses. A spread can apply to gross swap notional even when net risk-free funding resembles `(L-1)*rate`; do not automatically multiply the spread by only `L-1`. Physical holdings and other derivatives change this bridge. Filed swap P&L can contain financing/commissions that do not appear in the line called interest expense. If contract notional/exposure or spread basis is ambiguous, label the funding decomposition partial and preserve an identified all-in residual rather than inventing precision.
- Estimate residuals from actual NAV versus a matched current benchmark over identical reset periods. Label residuals as observed aggregate discrepancies, not an identified financing spread. Use holdings/contracts or filed statements to separate causes where possible. Separate public measured values, issuer targets, model estimates and unverified assumptions; publish ranges for unobserved spreads rather than disguising a point estimate as market evidence.
- Accrue costs using the contract's actual calendar/business-day convention. Convert annual NAV costs to per-reset debits explicitly. A constant 252-day debit is a disclosed approximation, not an observed fund expense path. Stress rate/spread changes and their joint behavior with market stress.

## 3. Model trend, variation, time and liquidation

For a linear-reset exposure, with benchmark return `r_t`, chosen leverage `L_t` and modeled NAV cost debit `c_t`, compute the actual discrete product `W_T/W_0 = product(1 + L_t*r_t - c_t)`. The equation is exact for the stipulated period net returns; the stipulated funding/tracking model can still be approximate. Use actual NAV products for historical fund outcomes. Model structural closure/stop/acceleration rules from the prospectus; do not allow negative wealth or spontaneous recovery after a modeled zero barrier. A theoretical `1+L*r-c <= 0` is an absorption stress, not a claim about the exact issuer liquidation process.

Always test jointly: smooth/low-variation uptrend; strong but volatile uptrend; flat/choppy round trips; persistent decline; crash and fast V-shaped recovery; decline followed by a delayed recovery; and jumps/rate-spread stress. Vary terminal benchmark gain, time to realize it and variation independently; include the strong bull case even when underwriting a cautious view. A 20% one-year scenario alone cannot represent a 100% quarterly rally. Never construct only a fixed terminal return and one high-volatility alternating path to infer current EV.

For positive wealth factors, report the exact identity `log(W_L) - L*log(W_1) = sum(log(1+L*r_t-c_t) - L*log(1+r_t))` at constant L. Explain the trend term and the path/friction correction. At L>1, smooth compounding can exceed `L * cumulative_return`; a lower wealth level than the idealized `W_1^L` can coexist with large outperformance of the unlevered fund. For two costless periods, `R_L-L*R_1=L*(L-1)*r_1*r_2`: same-sign periods improve this comparison, opposite-sign periods worsen it. For fixed constant leverage and the same paired return/cost observations, permuting their order leaves terminal wealth unchanged; ordering changes drawdown, investor cash flows, thresholds, dynamic leverage and state-dependent costs. Do not claim an additional terminal order penalty when the multiset is unchanged.

Historical standard deviation is a descriptive centered statistic, not a complete decay forecast. Inspect realized reset-period squared returns/quadratic variation, skew, jumps, serial dependence and local volatility clustering. Distinguish them from low-frequency trend dispersion. In a continuous diffusion approximation, `g_L approx L*g_1 - 0.5*L*(L-1)*sigma^2 - C`, equivalently `L*mu - 0.5*L^2*sigma^2 - C`. State arithmetic drift versus geometric growth definitions, units and diffusion assumptions. This is a diagnostic identity/approximation, not a replacement for discrete path calculation, a guaranteed breakpoint or proof that leverage always loses. For large reset-period moves, inverse exposure or nonlinear products, recompute exact outcomes.

Do not confuse this log/path relation with arithmetic expectation. Under independent identically distributed reset-period NET returns with finite mean, expected wealth is `(1+E[net_return])^N`; for valid linear returns and constant cost this is `(1+L*E[r]-c)^N`. Changing variance at fixed arithmetic drift can leave arithmetic expected wealth unchanged while reducing median/log wealth and worsening tails. Correlated returns, state-dependent costs, dynamic exposure and barriers require the full joint distribution. Never insert the expected benchmark terminal wealth into a cube-minus-variance formula and label the result expected leveraged wealth: in general `E[X^L] != E[X]^L`. Explicitly report how rare large bull outcomes contribute to the mean.

A larger variance alone does not universally order expected log wealth or medians across arbitrary fixed-mean distributions. A mean-preserving spread/convex-order argument can order expected concave log utility when its domain and integrability hold; median ordering requires additional distributional assumptions. State the distribution/model conditions rather than treating variance as a sufficient statistic.

## 4. Calibrate probabilities without selecting the conclusion

- Declare the estimand first: e.g., net 1/3/6/12-month return buying after an ex-ante drawdown condition. Freeze universe, start-condition features, lookbacks, horizons, exit rules and data cutoff before examining favorable results. Include all eligible rolling starts; report unconditional results and any present-state-conditioned results. Select matching states only using information available at each start, never a future trough, subsequent trend or eventual survival. Show feature ranges and distance of the present state from calibration coverage.
- Align benchmark regime/history; separately report live-fund history, matched current-index history and any hypothetical predecessor/backfill. Include failed/closed products where relevant and disclose survivorship, benchmark concentration/reconstitution, sample selection and index backtest bias. Do not silently splice predecessor returns into current-strategy performance.
- Use historical rolling outcomes as descriptive frequencies, not automatically today's forecast. Overlapping windows and lifecycle cohorts share observations: disclose starts, nonoverlapping/episode counts, effective sample limitations and uncertainty. Assess independent drawdown episodes and block/episode bootstrap confidence intervals; do not report thousands of overlapping starts as thousands of independent events. Disclose full-sample parameter fitting and hindsight mean-matching. Do not optimize matching thresholds or choose an attractive window without walk-forward/out-of-sample evaluation.
- Compare immediate entry, staged entry and explicitly defined waiting signals under identical contributions and opportunity costs. Test timing-rule rankings across market eras, start-date phases and nonoverlapping episodes; use paired uncertainty estimates. Label retrospective robustness checks as such; a later sample already inspected during development is not an untouched holdout.
- Use contiguous block or stationary bootstrap of joint benchmark returns and contemporaneous funding/spread/collateral rows where appropriate. Report block-length sensitivity and cross-block artifacts; a bootstrap preserves dependence only within sampled blocks and cannot create unseen crashes/regimes. Include synthetic jump/closure stresses separately. Do not independently reshuffle returns and funding when their dependence matters. An IID Gaussian/GBM is a transparent baseline, not the sole probability engine.
- If using regime-switching, stochastic-volatility or jump models, calibrate transitions, drift, volatility and correlations on dated training data, evaluate genuinely later periods, and include parameter/model uncertainty. Preserve positive-trend persistence and volatility clustering as well as bear/chop regimes; do not hard-code a pessimistic or optimistic regime mix. Check calibration stability across windows and present-state matching. If current drift/regime probabilities cannot be estimated credibly, state that point explicitly and report historical distributions, conditional sensitivities and break-even thresholds instead of claiming a precise current EV.
- Options-implied distributions are risk-neutral pricing distributions, not automatically physical outcome probabilities or a forecast EV. If using them, document the risk-premium/measure conversion and liquidity/maturity limitations, or retain them as pricing/stress inputs. Use historical contemporaneous rates for historical synthetic validation and separately sourced/assumed future rates for forward EV; today's rate is not the cost for every historical day.
- Keep independently justified fundamental/index-return forecasts distinct from technical path calibration. A strong semiconductor demand outlook does not establish index shareholder return at today's valuation or a fast recovery. Likewise, high historical leveraged returns do not prove unrealized alpha. Label each set of probabilities `historical frequency`, `calibrated model` or `analyst judgment`; never promote equal scenario weights to measured odds.

## 5. Measure investor outcomes and decision conditions

Publish by horizon and strategy: arithmetic expected terminal wealth/return; median wealth; expected log growth (minus infinity if positive-probability ruin); loss probability; probability/magnitude of losing 50/80/100%; 5/25/75/95% quantiles; lower-tail expected shortfall with its convention; maximum-drawdown distribution; recovery-time/censoring; and probability of beating the investable unlevered comparison. Distinguish `CAGR(E[wealth])`, `E[scenario CAGR]` and `exp(E[log wealth])/year`; the first can be dominated by rare bull tails. Report the share of expected terminal wealth contributed by the best 1% of outcomes and sensitivity to drift, trend duration, variation, funding, starting valuation and probability/model uncertainty. Compute log growth and losses of the actual whole portfolio, including cash and correlated holdings, as well as the fund: portfolio log utility is not a weighted average of component log utilities. For funded strategies, subtract all paid-in capital when reporting profit and separate contributions from investment returns; cash replenishment time is not investment recovery time. A positive arithmetic EV alone is not an adequate recommendation for a highly skewed fund.

Translate evidence into a clear conditional decision: which realistic regimes support buying, which destroy the thesis, and how much estimated payoff remains after frictions. Provide entry/exit rules using benchmark valuation/drawdown/forward-return and risk conditions rather than nominal fund share price or distance from its split-adjusted high alone. Distinguish a fundamental long-term sector thesis from an additional forecast of the path needed for leverage to outperform. Explain whether the proposed edge is information, interpretation, horizon or unproven. Present favorable and unfavorable probability/parameter sensitivities symmetrically; do not stack conservative haircuts into EV or tune weights to create a buy.

Size using loss/ruin tolerance, cash needs, correlation and total portfolio stress exposure; distinguish a fund's leverage multiple from whole-portfolio exposure. Kelly/optimal leverage requires credible return-distribution estimates and utility assumptions; do not default to a Kelly allocation from unstable historical drift or call it personalized advice. Elicit or independently justify utility/CRRA inputs before optimization; never tune risk aversion to recover a preferred allocation or equate stated high tolerance with a particular coefficient. If preferences are unelicited, report sensitivity and loss budgets, not a personalized optimum. A cash-funded fund share position ordinarily limits direct loss to invested capital, subject to its legal structure; margin borrowing creates separate obligations. A stop-loss is not guaranteed execution in a gap or halt. Analysis does not authorize trades, holdings edits or price-alert writes.

## 6. Execute and validate

Use [leveraged_funds.py](../scripts/leveraged_funds.py) for deterministic linear-reset products, observation-based metrics, joint moving-block sampling and past-only start selection. Run [test_leveraged_funds.py](../scripts/test_leveraged_funds.py) after modifying it. Its costs are explicit per-reset NAV debits; it supplies no fees, forecasts, probabilities or structural-product valuations. Use the corporate DCF branch only for material underlying-company valuation, not as a prerequisite for fund mechanics.

Validate identity/reset/benchmark/time alignment, adjusted-return versus NAV selection, costs exactly once, annualization conventions, distribution/quantile conventions, probability sum, zero barriers, same-terminal different-variation paths, favorable trends, inverse/reset variants, drawdowns and future-data exclusion. Reconcile a historical interval to issuer NAV performance and an independent implementation before claiming historical accuracy. Archive input hashes, code version, cutoff, calibration window, random seeds, sensitivity results and failed/omitted checks. Numerical tests prove arithmetic/contract behavior, not the truth of future drift, regime odds or funding spreads.

Use verified primary references as methodological anchors, without copying their illustrative parameters into a current forecast: [Avellaneda and Zhang, path/variance/financing decomposition](https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/LeveragedETF20090515.pdf); [Hsieh et al., dependence and compounding, research preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20116v1); [Leung, Park and Yeo, parameter-uncertain utility, peer-reviewed 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11579-024-00371-1) ([author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02084)); [Ayres and Nalebuff, lifecycle leverage, Journal of Portfolio Management 2013](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2013.39.2.073); [Vanguard, existing lump sums versus cost averaging, institutional research 2023](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf); [Politis and Romano, stationary bootstrap](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870); [Bailey et al., backtest selection](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf); and [FINRA, daily objectives and multiday holding](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/lowdown-leveraged-and-inverse-exchange-traded-products). Verify publication status, actual sample, horizon, calibration and assumptions before transferring a research result. Verify current product facts from the actual issuer and regulator rather than these general methods papers.

## Actionable inverse entry requirement

For a buy-now question, produce a decision and model-derived alert levels, not only distribution tables. Hold economic value assumptions explicit, solve the underlying entry level needed for the chosen net return and relative-return criteria, and map to the wrapper price from a dated anchor over alternative pre-entry paths. Pre-entry price decline and future drift are different inputs; a lower nominal price from reset decay need not improve forward economics. State expiry, split/revision invalidation and companion underlying conditions. A price alert can trigger this check without promising automatic execution. Where no realistic entry meets the conditions, show the inverse calculation or tested domain and why; do not use a legacy level as a substitute.
